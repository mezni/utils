# Product

## Register

product

## Users

**Primary — EV drivers in Tunisia.** They open the map to find nearby charging stations, check availability (how many free plugs), see the station's location and address, and navigate there. Their context is mobile or desktop, usually in transit or planning a trip. They want confidence the station exists, is working, and has a free spot.

**Secondary — Station partners and platform admins.** They use the dashboard to manage stations, monitor chargers, and oversee operations. Their workflow is desktop, data-heavy, and task-oriented.

## Product Purpose

BorneMap helps EV drivers in Tunisia discover and locate available charging stations with confidence. It aggregates station data, shows real-time availability, and gets drivers to a working charger — reducing range anxiety and making EV ownership practical in Tunisia. For partners, it provides the tools to keep station data accurate and operations running.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, calm, clear. The app should feel like a reliable co-pilot — it doesn't need to shout. The personality is quiet competence: precise information presented without friction. The brand is modern but not cold, professional but not corporate, reassuring without being paternal.

**Voice**: Direct, plain, helpful. Short sentences. No marketing hype. Verbs over adjectives.

## Anti-references

- **Generic corporate dashboards**: Overdesigned admin panels, dark-themed SaaS UIs, heavy cards-and-gradients, busy sidebars with icons everywhere.
- **Cold utility maps**: The sterile, blue-heavy Google Maps aesthetic. The map should feel approachable, not like infrastructure.

## Design Principles

1. **Driver-first clarity.** The map is the hero. Every decision makes finding a station faster, not the interface more interesting.
2. **Calm confidence.** Reassuring through precision, not volume. The UI steps back so the data leads. Quietly competent.
3. **Trust through accuracy.** Station status, charger availability, distance — every number must feel authoritative. No ambiguous indicators.
4. **Consistent across surfaces.** One visual language works for a full-screen map on a phone and a data dashboard on a widescreen monitor. Not two different apps that happen to share a logo.
5. **Practical over decorative.** Every element earns its place. No ornament that doesn't serve the user's task. Beautiful because it's well-made, not because it's adorned.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA as baseline. Improve iteratively toward AAA where practical. Key considerations: sufficient color contrast on map markers and labels (the map background varies), non-color status indicators for charger availability (pattern or icon in addition to color), and reduced-motion support for map transitions.
